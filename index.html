How Kyoto Stole My Heart

I'll never forget walking through the endless orange gates of Fushimi Inari early in the morning, before the crowds arrived. The only sounds were birds and my own footsteps. Later that week I stood in the Arashiyama bamboo grove listening to the wind move through the stalks, and I felt calmer than I had in years. Kyoto taught me that slowing down can be the best part of a trip.

"In Kyoto, even an ordinary street feels like it has a thousand years of stories."

Must-See Places

⛩️

Fushimi Inari Taisha

A mountain path lined with around 10,000 bright orange torii gates. Go at sunrise for the quietest walk.

🎋

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Towering bamboo that sways and creaks in the breeze. It feels like stepping into another world.

🏯

Kinkaku-ji

The Golden Pavilion, covered in gold leaf and reflected perfectly in the pond around it.

🏮

Gion District

Old wooden teahouses and lantern-lit lanes, where you might spot a geiko or maiko at dusk.

🍡

Nishiki Market

"Kyoto's Kitchen": five blocks of pickles, mochi, matcha sweets, skewers, and fresh seafood.

🍵

Tea Ceremony

Kyoto is the home of matcha from nearby Uji. A tea ceremony is a calm, beautiful ritual worth trying.

Why You'll Love It Too

  • Living history. Kyoto was Japan's capital for over 1,000 years and has more than 1,600 temples and 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites.
  • Peace and quiet. Zen rock gardens and mossy temple grounds make it easy to slow down and breathe.
  • Amazing food. From street snacks to kaiseki, a traditional multi-course meal, every bite is made with care.
  • Something for everyone. History lovers, foodies, photographers, and nature fans all find their favorite corner.
